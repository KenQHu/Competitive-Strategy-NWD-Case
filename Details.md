# Part B. Case design and detailed analysis

Part A above is the outline. Part B turns it into the presentation design and the full analysis behind every slide. 
*Tags: [Case] = case text or exhibit, [AR] = NWD Annual Report 2026 (PDF page), [SFF] = Sustainable Finance Framework 2022, [L] = lecture slide. "Calc" means we computed it from case figures. "Judgment" means a team assessment, not data.*

## B0. Case design: storyline and architecture

### Governing thought
Cheng satisfies the board by making ESG earn its place: fund what pays, finance what is financeable, cap what is philanthropic, protect K11, and deleverage through non-K11 assets.

### Three key messages (the pyramid)
1. **Diagnosis.** The 2023 squeeze comes from capital structure and asset mix, not from ESG. ESG spending is a fraction of one expense line, and the leakage to perpetual holders, tax and minorities is far larger.
2. **Prescription.** Run a three-part plan: (a) ESG triage using NWD's own eligibility test plus a returns test, (b) K11 protected and monetized capital-light, with non-K11 assets sold first, (c) a board financial gate with a 12-month review point.
3. **Proof.** The FY2026 epilogue validates the direction (liquidity was the real constraint, K11 became the monetizable asset) and exposes our blind spots (leverage was deeper than the 2023 case showed, and ESG ratings did not protect credit).

### Narrative arc
Tension (Slide 1) leads to diagnosis (2–4), then options and lens (5–7), then decision (8–10), then proof (11), then close (12). Each slide title is a full-sentence claim so a listener who reads only titles hears the whole argument.

### Speaker split (adjust to group size)
* **Speaker 1:** Slides 1–4 (story and numbers).
* **Speaker 2:** Slides 5–7 (assumptions, lens, options).
* **Speaker 3:** Slides 8–10 (solutions). With more members, split 8 / 9 / 10 across three people.
* **Speaker 4:** Slides 11–12 and lead on Q&A. Everyone prepares two Q&A topics (see B7).

### Design rules
* One claim per slide; the number that proves it goes in large type.
* Source footer on every slide (Exhibit number or AR/SFF page).
* Fixed colour code: green = profitable ESG, blue = brand-linked K11, grey = philanthropic. Reuse it on slides 6, 8 and 10.
* Every exhibit we show must be one we can defend line by line in Q&A.
* Keep the 2023 decision logic and the hindsight epilogue visibly separate (a distinct header band on slide 11).

## B1. Part 1 detailed analysis: background and key problem (Slides 1–4)

### Slide 1. The decision and the tension
**Facts from the case**
* **2 Jan 2023:** travel between Hong Kong and the Mainland was announced to resume, with full normalization on 6 Feb 2023. This opens the Greater Bay Area opportunity.
* **23 Feb 2023:** board meeting. The case frames two questions for Cheng: should ESG spending be cut to fuel post-pandemic investment, and could the ESG image built during COVID be a competitive advantage as travel patterns evolve?
* The case closes with the sharpest version: should Cheng stop deploying new ESG projects for at least one year, and if so, how does he improve financial health?
* The chairperson is Dr. Henry Cheng, Adrian Cheng's father [Case]. Cheng expects the board to prioritize financial sustainability for shareholders over ESG for other stakeholders [Case].

**Stakeholder map** *(Judgment, built from case facts)*

| Stakeholder | What they want | Evidence in case | Power over the decision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Board and chairperson** | Capital efficiency, shareholder returns | Cheng anticipates a financial-first board; board questions ESG vs expansion allocation | Decisive |
| **Cheng** | Protect long-term ESG and K11 vision | Believes ESG benefits society and company; K11 is pivotal | High, but must persuade |
| **Lenders and perpetual holders** | Stable cash flow and distributions | Perps took HK$2.38bn of FY22 profit; financing outflow HK$5.72bn | High, indirect |

*So what.* The decision is a stakeholder trade-off, but the binding constraint is the board's financial test. Our solution has to pass that test first, then protect the others.

**Reading the table**
* The operating-margin gap is already large (11.3% vs 42.1% and 26.4%), so the gap does not start at the net-income line.
* NWD has the best ESG risk score on all four lines and a middling controversy level, so better ESG did not produce better margins. Rivals earn more with worse ESG scores.
* This does not prove ESG does not matter. It shows ESG is not what separates NWD from peers in profitability.

**Hypotheses for the margin gap** *(Judgment; each needs verification before we state it as fact)*
1. **Business mix:** NWD's portfolio includes construction, roads, aircraft leasing, insurance, hotels and a department store operation (HK$1,318.4m of department store expenses) [Case], which tend to carry lower margins than pure property investment.
2. **Leakage below operating profit:** perpetual securities, non-controlling interests, joint-venture swings and a 53% tax charge.
3. Peer net income includes fair-value and joint-venture effects, so net margin overstates operating comparability. That is why we lead with operating margin.

*What to say aloud.* "NWD has the lowest ESG risk score of the three and the lowest margins. ESG is not what separates us from SHKP and Henderson."

### Slide 4. The key problem
**Problem statement.** "How can Cheng restore financial credibility with the board by February 2023, without dismantling the K11 and ESG franchise that differentiates NWD?"

**Decision criteria our solution must meet** *(Judgment)*

| Criterion | Test |
| :--- | :--- |
| Liquidity | Helps close the HK$1.5bn net cash gap without adding long-dated high-rate debt |
| Board credibility | Every ESG item justified in financial terms |
| Brand and advantage | K11 and the shared-value story survive |
| Reversibility | Can restart ESG when conditions improve |

**Framing from the lecture.** Defending value means defending the licence society grants a firm [L2]. ## B2. Part 2 detailed analysis: assumptions, lens and options (Slides 5–7)

*Evidence-time discipline.* Cheng decides in February 2023. The case, the 2022 Sustainable Finance Framework and the lecture were available to him. The 2026 Annual Report is hindsight. We use hindsight only to corroborate or to run the epilogue, and we label it.

### Slide 5. Three assumptions, each with a test

**Assumption A1: The board objects to unfunded ESG spending, not to ESG itself.**
* **If-then form:** if ESG spending is tied to financeable or revenue-linked outcomes, then the board will accept it. This drives Solution 3.
* **Evidence in the case:** Cheng "anticipated" that board members would prioritize financial sustainability over ESG for other stakeholders. The board raised concerns about how to split investment between expansion and ESG. The proposal being debated was a one-year pause on new projects while fulfilling existing commitments, so existing commitments stood and no one proposed abandoning ESG. The board also oversees ESG through a Sustainability Committee, and mandatory ESG training runs up to board level [Case].
* **Evidence from NWD's own framework (available in 2023):** the 2022 framework defines eligible projects as those supporting "profitable sustainable development" [SFF p.4], so NWD itself conditions ESG finance on profitability.
* **Weakness:** Cheng's expectation is his reading of the board, not a board statement. We flag it as an inference.
* **What would falsify it:** the board demanding cuts to existing, contractually committed ESG items.

**Assumption A2: Part of NWD's ESG activity is financeable, so it pays for itself or lowers funding cost.**
* **If-then form:** if projects meet recognized green or social criteria, then they can be funded through labelled instruments instead of the operating budget. This drives Solution 1.
* **Evidence available in 2023:** the framework lists eligible categories with hard thresholds: BEAM Plus Gold or above, LEED Gold or above, at least 20% energy-efficiency improvement, EV charging, water efficiency, circular economy, climate adaptation, affordable housing, access to health and education, and support for small businesses hit by disasters or pandemics [SFF p.5–8]. It covers NWD, New World China Land and K11 [SFF p.3]. The Renewable Energy Roadmap already formed the basis of NWD's sustainability-linked bonds [SFF p.3]. Sustainable financing is Stage 2 of NWD's own property lifecycle [Exhibit 5].
* **A useful feature:** existing financing can be designated under the framework if a material portion is still undrawn or unallocated, and proceeds can refinance existing debt on eligible projects [SFF p.4]. So the lever does not require new ESG spending.
* **Hindsight corroboration (AR p.89–90):** NWD had a sizeable book of labelled instruments, roughly HK$30bn+ by our tally of facility sizes, about a fifth of debt. The 2021 sustainability-linked bond targets were met.
* **Weakness:** neither source quantifies the pricing benefit (the "greenium"). Treat the size of the benefit as a hypothesis for NWD treasury to confirm.
* **What would falsify it:** NWD treasury showing no pricing difference between labelled and plain debt.

**Assumption A3: K11 is the ESG-linked asset with revenue and exit value.**
* **If-then form:** if K11 is protected and grown capital-light, then it supports rents, sales and funding. This drives Solution 2.
* **Evidence in the case:** Cheng founded K11 in 2008 and the K11 Art Foundation in 2010 and built two art villages in Wuhan. The "art x commerce" model combines a museum-retail concept with offices and residences. The chairperson called K11 the driving force of the group's stage-like shopping experience. Exhibit 6 lists distinct brand value as a strength. Exhibit 10 suggests selling old properties without K11 branding. K11 Musea is wholly owned [Exhibit 5]. Plans stood at 28 K11 projects by FY2026 [Case].
* **Weakness:** Exhibit 6 also flags rising receivables and the threat of tenants not paying rent. The 28-project plan is capital-hungry, so A3 supports protecting K11 and does not support unconditional expansion.
* **What would falsify it:** K11 assets showing no rent or sales premium over comparable malls.

## B3. Part 3 detailed analysis: solutions (Slides 8–10)

### Slide 8. Solution 1: four-gate ESG triage
*Design principle.* A label is not a return. Every ESG item passes a certification test (NWD's own) and a returns test.

| Gate | Question | If yes | If no |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Committed** | Is it contractual or already under way? | **Honor:** continue to completion | Go to Gate 2 |
| **2. Eligible** | Does it meet the framework thresholds (BEAM Plus or LEED Gold or above, at least 20% energy improvement, or another listed category)? | **Finance:** designate or re-designate to labelled funding, using the framework's rule for existing transactions | Go to Gate 3 |
| **3. Earns its keep** | Does it have a measurable payback, financing benefit or revenue link within a hurdle (proposal: about five years, to be calibrated with NWD)? | **Earn:** fund from operations with a named owner | Go to Gate 4 |
| **4. Partner-fundable** | Can a partner, donor or social-bond structure carry it? | **Re-route:** shift funding off NWD's balance sheet | **Defer:** pause for 12 months |

**Where the portfolio lands (from Slide 6)**
* **Honor:** existing Build for Good commitments, committed pilots, in-progress certifications.
* **Finance:** certified green buildings, retrofits above the 20% threshold, renewable energy items, water and climate adaptation.
* **Earn:** K11 features with a rent or sales link, tenant green engagement.
* **Re-route:** Share for Good, Share a Meal, art foundation activities.
* **Defer:** new events, new pilots, new philanthropic launches, expansion of reporting databases.

**Cash effect (Illustrative; see the sensitivity table in Slide 2).** The deferred bucket frees roughly HK$0.2–1.2bn depending on how much of selling and marketing is ESG. That is 16%–79% of the FY22 net cash gap, so even the 50% case leaves about a fifth of the gap open. So Solution 1 is a discipline-and-credibility lever, and Solutions 2 and 3 carry the liquidity weight.

**Reputation safeguards** *(the Exhibit 10 risks of diminished brand, customer backlash and lost edge)*
* Frame it as "focus and finish": announce completion of existing commitments, not retreat.
* Keep high-visibility, low-cost community items alive through partners.
* Treat reputation risk as pre-emptive, not restorative [L98–99]; a high-salience brand pays more for a misstep.
* The broader ESG backlash [L5] gives cover for a disciplined message.

### Slide 9. Solution 2: protect K11 and fund the plan with non-K11 disposals
**(a) Protect.** K11 is the one ESG-linked asset with a commercial engine. Exhibit 6 names brand value as a strength and the museum-retail concept ties art to rents and sales. Cutting it would remove the differentiation the case highlights.

**(b) Grow capital-light.** The 28-project plan by FY2026 is incompatible with a cash squeeze. Proposals (Judgment):
* Stage-gate each project: proceed only with pre-leasing, partner capital or an agreed exit route.
* Use partner structures. NWD already carries large joint-venture (HK$48.7bn) and associate (HK$16.2bn) interests [Exhibit 9], so the model exists.
* Sell minority stakes in stabilized K11 assets while keeping the brand and management role.
* Pursue sustainability-linked lease terms and tenant engagement, which the framework already supports for eligible green buildings [SFF p.5].

**(c) Fund with non-K11 disposals first.** Exhibit 10 proposes identifying older property without K11 branding for sale. The balance sheet has the room [Exhibit 9]: investment properties of HK$211.2bn, properties held for sale of HK$21.8bn and properties under development of HK$62.1bn.

**Disposal sizing** *(Calc, HK$m, on the FY22 investment-property book of 211,220.7; assumed share sold and assumed discount to book)*

| Share of book sold | At book | 10% discount | 20% discount | 30% discount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0.5%** | 1,056 | 951 | 845 | 739 |
| **1%** | 2,112 | 1,901 | 1,690 | 1,479 |
| **2%** | 4,224 | 3,802 | 3,380 | 2,957 |
| **3%** | 6,337 | 5,703 | 5,069 | 4,436 |

Selling about 1% of the book, even at a 20% discount, roughly covers the whole HK$1.53bn FY22 net cash gap. The share of the book that is non-K11 and saleable is unknown, and the table ignores lost rental income because the case gives no yields. State both limits aloud.

**(d) Risks and mitigations** (Exhibit 10)

| Risk | Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Few buyers with cash | Run staged tranches and several parallel processes |
| Low prices right after COVID | Set a floor tied to independent valuation; sell the most liquid assets first |
| Brand and investor-confidence damage from selling malls | Sell only non-K11, non-flagship assets; pair each sale with a clear deleveraging message |
| Lost recurring income | Choose low-yield or non-core assets; track pro-forma EBITDA before each sale |

*Why not borrow more.* Exhibit 10 warns of locking in high 2022 rates and struggling to repay if revenue does not grow. Revenue was flat in FY22 [Exhibit 9], so there is no organic repayment engine yet.

### Slide 10. Solution 3: the financial gate, dashboard and board communication
**(1) Extend the existing panel into a financial gate.** The framework already creates a Sustainable Finance Review Panel with senior people from Finance and Accounts, Group Audit and Management Services, and Sustainability. It recommends eligible projects to the CFO or CEO and the chair of the Group Sustainability Steering Committee, and decisions are reported to the Board-level Sustainability Committee [SFF p.9]. We add: for the next 12 months, any new ESG commitment needs Finance sign-off against the Slide 8 gates, and exceptions go to the board. No new structure is needed.

**(2) Integrated ESG-to-finance dashboard.** The framework already specifies the ESG indicators (kWh, tCO2e, m³, tonnes of waste, certification level, beneficiaries) [SFF p.11]. We pair each with a financial KPI.

| ESG indicator | Paired financial KPI | Why the board cares |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Energy reduction (kWh) | HK$ energy cost saved; payback period | Direct operating-cost relief |
| Certification level achieved | Eligibility for labelled funding; occupancy and rent versus comparable buildings | Funding cost and revenue |
| Water and waste reduction | Operating cost per square foot | Cost efficiency |
| Tenant green engagement | Tenant retention; rent collection | Exhibit 6 flags inability to collect rent as a threat |
| K11 traffic and sales per sq ft | Net operating income of K11 assets | Proves the brand premium |
| Beneficiaries (social programmes) | Cost per beneficiary; share funded by partners | Shows the philanthropic cap is working |
| External ESG ratings (MSCI, CDP, S&P) | Whether they affect financing eligibility | Prevents paying for ratings that change no terms |

*These pairings are proposals; the thresholds need NWD's data.*

**(3) Review gate and restart criteria (proposal).** At 12 months, restart new ESG projects if (a) net cash change has turned positive, (b) the disposal programme has reached its target, and (c) Finance can show a financing or revenue benefit for the proposed projects. The board then sees ESG as conditional, not abandoned.

**(4) Incentive alignment.** ESG KPIs sit in appraisals and can influence bonuses [Case]. A pure ESG KPI rewards spending, so each ESG KPI gets a financial counterpart (cost, return, funding source).

**(5) Board paper structure.** Three pages: what we stop and why, what we keep and the financial case, what we need from the board. Use capital-allocation language, not "cost of ESG".

| Board concern [Case] | Our answer | Proof the board can track |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Investment split between expansion and ESG | Four-gate triage and a financial gate | Share of ESG spend passing the gates |

## B5. Implementation roadmap, risks and the 30-day diagnostic

**First 30 days: close the information gap**
The case gives no liabilities (Exhibit 9 lists only assets). Before any disposal or ESG decision is final, Finance should produce:
* total debt, maturity profile, interest rates and covenants;
* call dates and distribution terms on the perpetual securities;
* the share of selling and marketing that is ESG-related;
* the non-K11, saleable portion of the investment-property book with yields;
* pricing evidence for labelled versus plain debt.

These numbers either confirm the plan's scale or change it. Saying so openly is a strength in Q&A.

## B6. Part 4 detailed analysis: the real-world epilogue (Slide 11)

*Header band on the slide: "Hindsight. Not what Cheng knew in February 2023."*

**Scorecard: our claims against what happened**

| Our claim (2023 view) | What happened | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ESG is not the cause of the squeeze** | FY2026 loss of HK$26.8bn, driven by 11 SKIES (HK$18.3bn impairments and provisions) and about HK$8.0bn of property impairments [AR p.179] | **Validated** |
| **Liquidity is the binding constraint** | 74% of HK$143.3bn debt due within three years (HK$106.6bn); HK$23.2bn due within a year; cash HK$17.0bn [AR p.184, p.8]; the auditor treated going-concern assessment as a key audit matter [AR p.189] | **Validated** |
| **K11 is the monetizable asset** | Shanghai K11 C-REIT (about RMB3.2bn, roughly HK$3.7bn), with the brand kept; new perpetuals tied to Victoria Dockside; a bank facility secured on it [AR p.183–184] | **Validated**, with a warning: K11 also became collateral |
| **Cut cost and capex, sell assets** | G&A down 19%; capex HK$11.8bn; first recurring profit in three years (HK$2.2bn) [AR p.8] | **Partly validated:** net debt still rose HK$6.2bn and net gearing reached 68.3% from 58.1% |
| **Pause new ESG for a year** | The AR does not show whether NWD paused. It did keep its science-based targets, met the sustainability-linked bond targets, and introduced a Social Value Framework [AR p.58, 89] | **Not testable** |
| **Labelled finance helps** | A sizeable labelled book, but HK$4.77bn of green loans were allocated to 11 SKIES, now written down [AR p.90]; ESG ratings improved (CDP "A", GRESB five-star) while credit deteriorated [AR p.97] | **Mixed:** supports our returns test, undercuts any "ESG shields credit" claim |
| **(Blind spot)** the 2023 case hid the scale of leverage and project risk | See above; news reports add that Cheng resigned in September 2024, Bain is reportedly exploring an investment, and a roughly US$991m bond exchange offer was launched in October 2026 | **Our main gap** |

**What the epilogue teaches** *(to say aloud)*
1. The crisis was about debt, impairments and one outsized project, not ESG budgets, so a pause alone could never have solved it.
2. The direction held: liquidity first, K11 as the asset lenders and investors value.
3. Ratings are not a shield; returns discipline on labelled projects matters.
4. What we would add with hindsight: an early project-risk review for large flagship builds, earlier work on perpetual securities, and a partner or equity option.

**Hindsight rules for the team**
* Say "consistent with" and "validates the direction", never "we predicted".
* Keep the 2023 logic and the epilogue on separate slides.
* Give each AR figure its page tag; unsourced numbers invite challenge.
* Treat news-report items as developing and label them as such.
